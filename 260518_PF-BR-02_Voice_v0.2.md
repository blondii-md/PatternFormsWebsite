# PatternForms — Voice

## 1. Voice in one line

Pick one as canonical for the brand kit:

- **A.** Inventive, precise, building in public.
- **B.** Considered. Ambitious. Engineered.
- **C.** Research-led, plain-spoken, forward.
- **D.** A studio voice with an engineer's discipline.

> [!NOTE]
> Open decision **V-06**. The one-liner is also a quick gut-check tool: when a draft drifts, the one-liner tells you what it's drifted from.

PatternForms sounds like an atelier with an engineer's discipline — research-led, technically literate, ambitious about the work, plain-spoken about the stage we're at. We borrow some of the intelligence and aspiration of the tech-startup world without losing the design-and-engineering credibility that defines us.

---

## 2. Voice principles

Five rules that govern how we write.

### 2.1 Show the system, not the slogan

We earn trust by exposing how the thing works, not by performing certainty. When we describe a capability, we describe the mechanism behind it. A BOM is a BOM. A tolerance is a number. A pathway is a documented sequence. The substance is the marketing.

### 2.2 Precise — and ambitious

We use the specific noun, the specific number, the specific part. Precision is the aesthetic. But precision doesn't mean small — we're allowed to write about big ideas, frontier problems, and the scale of what's possible, as long as the specifics are real. Vision sits on top of evidence, never instead of it.

### 2.3 Invent in public

PatternForms is a research and design operation. We are inventing things — methods, parts, conventions, products. We say so. We share what's been built, what's being prototyped, and what's still in the lab. Iteration is part of the brand, not a flaw to hide.

### 2.4 Tech-fluent, architecturally credible

We are equally comfortable in two registers — the technical, system-thinking register of software and manufacturing, and the considered, material-aware register of architecture. We borrow the intelligence and ambition of the best tech writing without losing design discipline. We don't sound like a SaaS landing page; we don't sound like an architect's monograph; we sound like a studio that takes both crafts seriously.

### 2.5 Style matters

Our products, designs, and outputs are refined, pragmatic, and clever. Style is not decoration — it is a signal of capability. We pay attention to how things look and feel. We expect our writing to do the same: well-formed sentences, well-laid-out documents, well-considered word choices.

## 3. Tone — do / don't

The PatternForms voice sits in the same neighbourhood as **Candour.cc**, **Facit Homes**, **Gropyus**, **Arkit**, and **Kope** — confident, technically literate, designed. Not as restrained as a pure engineering firm; not as performative as a typical startup. Calibrate against this peer band.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Considered | Pretentious |
| Confident | Hyped |
| Ambitious | Grandiose |
| Inventive | Gimmicky |
| Forward | Breathless |
| Candid | Confessional |
| Precise | Pedantic |
| Systemic | Reductive |
| Architectural | Decorative |
| Designed | Over-designed |
| Direct | Curt |
| Tech-fluent | Tech-bro |

Dry humour and understatement are welcome. Marketing-speak and industry clichés are not.

## 4. Vocabulary

### 4.1 Words and phrases we use

**System words:** system, methodology, framework, logic, convention, rule, interface, tolerance, sequence, configuration, version.

**Build words:** part, sub-assembly, assembly, module, component, kit, fabrication, manufacturing, deployment, chassis.

**Discipline words:** considered, precise, specific, defined, documented, traceable, repeatable, predictable, resolved.

**R&D and innovation words:** research, invent, design, develop, prototype, iterate, ship, frontier, ambition, discipline, breakthrough *(used carefully — only when the substance warrants it)*.

**Industry-correct terms:** DfMA, MMC, KoP, BOM, BOO, EBOM, MBOM, PLM, PDM, configurator, parametric, Basis of Design (BoD).

**Product words:** dwelling (preferred over "home" in PatternForms contexts — "house" and "home" are reserved for PatternHouse), chassis, envelope, fit-out.

### 4.2 Words and phrases we avoid

| Avoid | Why | Use instead |
|---|---|---|
| Game-changing | Empty. | Describe the change. |
| Disrupt / disruption | Overused. | A precise description of what we do differently. |
| World-class / best-in-class | Unverifiable. | A measurable claim or comparison with evidence. |
| Unlock | Startup cliché. | Enable, allow, make possible. |
| Synergy | Corporate jargon. | The specific relationship between things. |
| Solutions provider | SaaS-speak. | What we actually deliver. |
| Cutting-edge / next-gen | Empty technology signal. | A specific capability with evidence. |
| Dream home | Property-developer cliché. | Dwelling, residence, the build. |
| Signature / bespoke (loosely) | Doesn't fit a product company. | Configurable, specified, selected. |
| Reimagine | Empty. | Redesign, rebuild, restructure. |
| Holistic | Vague. | Name the integrated parts. |
| Ecosystem | Overused metaphor. | System, network, supply chain — pick the correct one. |

**Use carefully (allowed when earned):**

- **Innovation / innovative** — fine when paired with a specific innovation.
- **Revolutionary** — once, for a real reason.
- **Visionary** — applied to work, rarely to ourselves.
- **Pioneering** — only with substance behind it.

### 4.3 Naming and capitalisation

- **PatternForms** — always one word, capital P and F. URL form `patternforms` acceptable.
- **PatternHouse**, **PatternWalls**, **PatternPassive** — same pattern. Never spaced or hyphenated.
- **Patterns** as a generic descriptor — lowercase. Reserve capital P-Pattern only for branded use.
- **DfMA**, **MMC**, **KoP**, **BOM**, **BOO** — all caps; expand on first use in public-facing copy, then acronym only.
- **R&D** — capitalised, with ampersand, no spaces. Always.

## 5. Sentence patterns

### 5.1 Sentence-level rules

- **Lead with the noun.** "The chassis is laminated timber" beats "What makes PatternForms different is laminated timber chassis."
- **Use specifics.** A number, a name, a part is always stronger than an adjective.
- **Short to medium length.** 8–20 words. Vary rhythm; avoid long architectural sentences with embedded clauses.
- **Active voice by default.** Passive only when the agent is genuinely irrelevant.
- **One idea per sentence.** Split when in doubt.

### 5.2 Paragraph rhythm

- **Open with the claim, then the substance.** Don't bury the point.
- **Short paragraphs.** Two to four sentences. Single-sentence paragraphs are allowed for emphasis.
- **Use lists when the content is genuinely enumerable.** Don't fake structure to look organised.

### 5.3 Contractions and tone flex

- Contractions are fine ("we're", "don't", "it's") in web, social, and conversational copy.
- Avoid contractions in technical documentation, reports, and formal partner correspondence.
- Dry humour is welcome where it lands. Forced humour is worse than none.

## 6. Voice by context

### 6.1 Website

- Headline copy: short, declarative, system-led, occasionally ambitious. The hero is allowed a bigger claim than the body.
- Body copy: precise, paragraph-form, occasional list. Conversational but not casual.
- Technical pages: more rigorous, more numbered, more diagrammatic.

### 6.2 Pitch deck

- Headline per slide is a declarative claim. The slide body proves it.
- Numbers wherever possible. Mechanisms before benefits.
- Acknowledge stage honestly — investors trust companies that are candid about what's not yet built.

### 6.3 Report / whitepaper

- Formal register. Full sentences, full names, expanded acronyms on first use.
- Structured headings, numbered sections, references to source documents (e.g. PF-BR-01, PH-XX).
- Conclusions stated plainly. No editorial flourishes.

### 6.4 LinkedIn and industry thought-leadership

Thought leadership is a **posture we are growing into**, not a current claim. Write accordingly.

- Lead with a specific observation, claim, or piece of work — not a generalisation.
- Show working — drawings, models, parts, tests, source documents. The system is the content.
- One claim per post, supported by evidence (image, number, link). No motivational quotes.
- First-person plural ("we", "PatternForms") for company posts; first-person singular when a founder is clearly attributed.
- Frequency over volume. Two substantial posts a month beat eight thin ones.
- Open-source the convention where it doesn't compromise IP — the PH-XX series, the BOM logic, the BoD framework can be discussed publicly without giving away product detail.

### 6.5 Social — broader

- Same rules as LinkedIn, lower formality where the platform allows.
- Visual-first formats — drawings, parts, fabrication, assembly photography.

### 6.6 Technical documentation

- The most precise register. Voice yields to clarity.
- Match the existing PF / PH document conventions (YAML frontmatter, document IDs, decision IDs, version history).
- Defer poetic language entirely.

### 6.7 Email and partner correspondence

- Cordial, direct, concise. State the ask and the deadline.
- Match the recipient's register without losing the PatternForms baseline.

## 7. Examples — before / after

### Example 1 — Website hero

**Before (wrong):**
> We're revolutionising the way Australians build homes — combining cutting-edge architecture with next-gen manufacturing to unlock the dream of beautiful, affordable housing for all.

**After (right):**
> A building-systems atelier. We develop the methodology, parts, and IP behind a new kind of construction. The first product line is in development; the system is the work.

---

### Example 2 — Capability description

**Before (wrong):**
> Our holistic approach empowers builders and architects alike with a seamless, end-to-end ecosystem.

**After (right):**
> PatternForms operates as two arms — a studio (architecture, engineering, design, management) and a supply line (parts, fabrication, manufacturing) — with R&D running through both. Architects specify within the system. Builders assemble what's supplied. The system gets sharper with every deployment.

---

### Example 3 — Honest about stage

**Before (wrong):**
> PatternForms is the world's most advanced productised building company, redefining what's possible in residential construction.

**After (right):**
> PatternForms is early. The BOM convention is in place; the first product line, PatternHouse, is in development; the methodology is documented and growing. We're inventing in public, and the work is the proof.

---

### Example 4 — Technical claim

**Before (wrong):**
> Industry-leading precision and quality.

**After (right):**
> Part tolerance ±1 mm; module tolerance ±3 mm. Verified per a defined measurement protocol (in development — see PF-REC R-F1).

---

### Example 5 — LinkedIn post

**Before (wrong):**
> 🚀 So excited to share our latest innovation in modular housing! Big things coming! Stay tuned! #PropTech #ModularHousing #FutureOfHousing

**After (right):**
> Stand-frame v3 off the CNC this week. 12 parts, four fixings per node, ±1 mm tolerance. The deeper change isn't the frame — it's that the BOM line for the part now matches the cut file. Small step in a longer arc. [photo]

---

### Example 6 — Thought-leadership opening

**Before (wrong):**
> The construction industry is broken. It's time for a revolution.

**After (right):**
> Construction's project-by-project model has a stakeholder problem: every party prices for their own uncertainty, and the uncertainties compound. There's a different way to set it up. Here's how PatternForms is doing it. [link to longer piece]

## 8. Voice for non-PatternForms-employees

The same rules apply when ghost-writing for the founders or partners. Adjust register slightly for the speaker, but never break the principles. Miyan tends to write with more ambition and vision; Rob tends to write with more pragmatism and structural grounding. Both are PatternForms voice — both stay inside the same five principles.
